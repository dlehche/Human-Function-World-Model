# Purpose-Asymmetric, Operationally Bidirectional Medicine–Human-Function Reasoning

[中文](MEDICINE_FUNCTION_BIDIRECTIONAL.zh-CN.md) · [Public explanatory essay](../HFWM_AND_MEDICINE.md)

HFWM connects medicine and human function without collapsing one into the other.

The relation is **asymmetric in purpose and bidirectional in operation**.

## Foundational relationship

Human function is a bodily reality that exists independently of medicine. Medicine did not create the body’s capacities for regulation, defense, repair, recovery, adaptation, mobility, communication, self-care, work, or participation.

Medicine is a human knowledge and professional-action system for understanding and managing health, disease, injury, bodily structure, physiological and pathological processes, and risk.

The enduring modeled entity in HFWM is the same whole person. Human function is the organizing coordinate, not the whole of personhood and not the boundary of the modeled world.

Medicine ultimately serves the person. At the bodily level, a central purpose of medicine is to protect, restore, support, compensate for, or maintain human function. This does not mean that every medical action immediately maximizes every function. A medical action may accept short-term functional loss to protect life, control greater harm, or preserve more important long-term function.

## Medicine’s direct objects and ultimate service

Medicine may directly act on:

- disease and injury;
- infection, tumors, bleeding, fracture, obstruction, or other pathology;
- bodily structure and physiological processes;
- acute and chronic risk;
- diagnostic uncertainty;
- medication, surgery, devices, monitoring, and other regulated medical actions.

These direct objects are not the ultimate person-level ends of medicine. HFWM therefore asks whether medical action protected life, reduced harm and suffering, preserved or restored capacity, changed actual engagement, altered recovery and boundary, and improved the person’s ability to live.

When full restoration is not possible, medicine may still preserve remaining capacity, reduce suffering, support dignity, and improve life.

## Medicine → human function

Medical evidence and medical action may constrain or change:

- safety and admissible action;
- structural and physiological conditions;
- internal-task activation and Demand;
- current human-function capacity;
- Actual Engagement Process and engagement boundary;
- recovery, reserve, and cost;
- feasibility of external tasks and participation.

Diagnosis, disease stage, injury, surgery, medication, imaging, laboratory findings, medical restrictions, red flags, and safety clearance remain typed medical evidence. They do not become human-function judgments merely by entering HFWM.

Medical clearance does not establish complete human-function recovery. A person may be medically permitted to act while still lacking the capacity, engagement, reserve, or recovery required for the real task.

## Human function → medicine

Human-function evidence may contribute to medicine by:

- showing the person-level meaning of disease or injury;
- clarifying treatment and recovery goals;
- documenting current capacity, actual engagement, cost, reserve, boundary, and lived-task impact;
- informing tolerance and recovery considerations around medical action;
- evaluating whether treatment produced meaningful person-level change;
- identifying new or progressive change that requires medical reassessment.

A governed medical question or escalation need may arise when:

- a previous functional explanation no longer explains the observed change;
- deterioration is rapid, progressive, or unexplained;
- recovery is abnormal or substantially delayed;
- previously stable task tolerance or capacity declines unexpectedly;
- new safety signals appear;
- required evidence or judgment lies outside the current professional scope.

Functional change is not an autonomous medical diagnosis. HFWM may generate a governed medical question, preserve the evidence and uncertainty, and route the person for medical reassessment. The resulting medical evidence is then written back into the same person model.

The absence of an obvious functional problem does not establish the absence of medical risk.

## The body’s endogenous role in disease response and recovery

The body is not represented as a passive object waiting for treatment.

Disease, infection, injury, surgery, and endogenous change may trigger governed internal tasks involving:

- defense;
- hemostasis and damage control;
- homeostatic regulation;
- tissue protection and repair;
- restoration of usable capacity;
- recovery and adaptation.

Disease, infection, injury, and surgery are not themselves those internal tasks. They may act as trigger, context, or constraint. The task generates or specifies Demand; required human-function capacities are then engaged through the Actual Engagement Process.

“Self-healing” is therefore not represented as one opaque force. Where governed identities and evidence are available, HFWM separates the relevant internal tasks, Demand, capacities, physiological realization, actual engagement, observed response, and longitudinal outcome.

Medicine may remove pathology, control threats, repair structure, support recovery conditions, or support, compensate for, and temporarily substitute for some bodily functions. Many processes of defense, regulation, repair, recovery, and adaptation must still be performed by the body itself.

## Architectural boundary

The medicine–human-function relation is bidirectional but not symmetric equivalence:

- medical diagnosis is not a human-function judgment;
- human-function change is not a disease diagnosis;
- medical evidence retains its medical identity;
- human-function evidence retains its functional identity;
- evidence, safety, professional authority, consent, and scope mediate both directions;
- HFWM does not autonomously select medication, surgery, or other high-risk medical treatment;
- human function does not exhaust the person’s values, identity, relationships, preferences, or dignity.

HFWM’s role is to preserve these distinctions while keeping the same person continuous across disease, treatment, endogenous recovery, rehabilitation, real life, maintenance, and later change.
