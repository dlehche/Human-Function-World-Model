# Abstract

Human knowledge is commonly partitioned by organ, disease, physiological
process, task, profession, intervention, and data system, even though
these phenomena occur in the same changing person. This paper presents
the Human Function World Model (HFWM), a whole-person world-model
framework that uses human function as the organizing coordinate for
representing the same person across internal and external demands over
time. Human function retains the UOHF root definition: the body's
capacity to be appropriately engaged to meet internal and external
demands. HFWM separates Demand, required human-function capacity,
current capacity, the Actual Engagement Process, observed response,
evidence-bearing assertions and inferences, governed action, and
longitudinal update. Governed internal and external task representations
make Demand explicit without replacing it; disease, injury, infection,
surgery, and endogenous change may trigger internal tasks such as
defense, regulation, repair, recovery, and adaptation but are not
themselves tasks. The public UOHF capacity coordinate system contains 18
core and 104 specific human functional capacities. A governed Functional
Bridge knowledge-relation layer connects these capacities to anatomical
and physiological reference knowledge, including the Human Reference
Atlas and NIH Whole Person Reference Physiome, without treating general
knowledge as person-specific evidence. HFWM further supports
bidirectional medicine-human-function reasoning, dynamic professional
and life coordination, governed report projections, and
history-preserving state update. Formal requirements traceability and
worked competency cases show architectural conformance for state,
observation, memory, uncertainty, action conditioning, routing, and
update; calibrated future-state prediction and causal counterfactual
planning remain empirical research targets.

# Historical Context and Scope

The term Human Function World Model (HFWM) was publicly disclosed by the
same author in UOHF Definition 2.0 on 15 July 2026, where UOHF, the
Individual Human Function Model, and HFWM were separated and HFWM was
characterized by time, action, transition, prediction, and memory
write-back [11]. UOHF Definition 2.1 subsequently generalized the root
definition to internal and external demands and continued this
development [1]. A targeted public search completed on 29 August 2026
using exact and near-exact terms including "Human Function World Model,"
"world model of human function," and "human functional world model" did
not identify an earlier independent dedicated framework with the same
organizing scope. This bibliographic observation is provisional and is
not used as evidence for the scientific validity of HFWM; discovery of
earlier equivalent work should revise the historical record.

# 1. Introduction

Modern knowledge of the human body is extraordinarily detailed but
institutionally fragmented. Anatomy describes structure; physiology
describes processes of maintenance and regulation; medicine identifies
disease, injury, pathological mechanisms, and safety; physical therapy
and occupational therapy restore activity and participation through
different professional scopes; speech-language and audiology services
address communication, swallowing, hearing, and related function;
psychology and psychotherapy address mental and behavioral conditions;
nutrition science addresses material and energy conditions; exercise
science develops capacity, tolerance, skill, and performance. These
bodies of knowledge are indispensable. The problem is that they do not
automatically preserve one shared computational object when they
encounter the same person.

A person, however, is not partitioned by professional jurisdiction.
Infection, circulation, sleep, walking, cognition, emotion, swallowing,
work, caregiving, recovery, and exercise occur in the same living
individual. If the scientific model changes its root object every time
the person changes professional setting, then longitudinal reasoning
becomes a collection of disconnected records rather than a model of the
person.

HFWM starts from a different commitment: the same whole person remains
the persistent modeled entity across internal physiology, disease and
injury, real-world activity, professional intervention, environment, and
time. Human function provides the operational coordinate that reconnects
these domains. The question is not merely what disease or measurement is
present, but what the person must currently accomplish, which human
functions are required, what capacities are available, how those
capacities actually operate, what cost and boundaries appear, what
evidence supports the judgment, what action is admissible now, and how
the person changes afterward.

This perspective also changes the meaning of recovery. After disease or
injury, the body is not simply a passive object whose physiological
variables drift toward baseline. It is actively meeting internal
biological goals. Where governed task identities and trigger rules are
available, HFWM represents these through internal tasks such as defense,
hemostasis, regulation, repair, restoration of homeostasis, recovery,
and adaptation. At the same time, the person must progressively resume
external tasks such as self-care, communication, mobility, family
responsibilities, work, and exercise. A whole-person world model must
represent both directions within one longitudinal architecture.

The contribution of this paper is therefore not another ontology paper
and not another static catalog of human functions. UOHF already provides
the semantic and formal foundation [1-4]. UOHF Definition 2.0 first
separated UOHF, the Individual Human Function Model, and HFWM and
defined HFWM as the layer adding time, action, transition, prediction,
and memory write-back [11]; Definition 2.1 subsequently generalized
the root semantics to internal and external demands [1]. The present
paper consolidates and expands that trajectory into a dedicated Human
Function World Model framework, specifies its modeled entity, state,
operational task structure, dynamics, action space, professional/life
coordination, and human-readable projections, and provides an initial
formal conformance evaluation together with explicit empirical
validation targets.

# 2. Aim, Method, and Scope

## 2.1 Aim

The aim is to define the minimum architecture required for a
scientifically defensible Human Function World Model whose modeled
subject is the same whole person across biological, medical, functional,
behavioral, environmental, professional, and longitudinal contexts.

## 2.2 Method

The paper uses conceptual analysis, type separation, formal modeling,
competency-question design, comparison with two major whole-person
initiatives, worked-case testing, formal conformance analysis, failure
analysis, and implementation-boundary analysis. It consolidates prior
public UOHF definitions and formal results [1-4] while avoiding
disclosure of production prompts, provider context assembly, database
schemas, proprietary retrieval pipelines, private relation topology,
operational thresholds, or other commercial implementation details.

## 2.3 Scope

HFWM is presented as a whole-person world-model framework, not as a
claim that every molecular, cellular, organ-level, behavioral, and
social degree of freedom has already been simulated. “Whole person”
means that the enduring subject remains the same person across scales
and contexts and that relevant knowledge can be attached to that person
without changing the root identity. Representational closure and
explicit unknowns are the target; omniscience is not.

# 3. Positioning Relative to Whole-Person Initiatives

## 3.1 NIH Whole Person Reference Physiome

The U.S. National Institutes of Health launched the Whole Person
Reference Physiome effort to build an integrated knowledge network of
healthy physiological function and ultimately an interactive model of
whole-person health [5]. The associated Research and Coordination
Center describes a multi-tier effort to curate an integrated map of the
healthy male and female body and transform it into models for analyzing
clinical data [6]. HFWM treats this work as complementary rather than
competitive. WPP can supply increasingly rich multiorgan and multiscale
physiological models that constrain or explain the whole-person state,
capacities, endogenous regulation, and recovery represented in HFWM.

The architectural distinction is the organizing question. WPP is
centered on integrated physiology and healthy whole-person function.
HFWM is centered on how the same person meets internal and external
tasks through human-function capacities and their actual engagement, and
how evidence, professional/life action, and feedback update that model.
A future implementation may directly incorporate WPP-compatible
physiological components without turning physiology into a separate
world.

## 3.2 European Virtual Human Twins

The European Commission defines a Virtual Human Twin (VHT) as a digital
representation of a human health or disease state, potentially spanning
cells, tissues, organs, and organ systems, built from software models
and data to mimic and predict the behavior of physical counterparts
[7]. The EDITH roadmap advances an integrated, multi-scale,
multi-time, multi-discipline vision intended to support personalized,
predictive, and participatory healthcare [8,9].

HFWM is compatible with that trajectory but makes a different organizing
commitment. A VHT may be built around health or disease states and
pathophysiological models. HFWM binds those states to the person's
internal and external task representations, required human-function
capacities, actual engagement processes and evidence-bearing engagement
assertions, evidence status, boundaries, professional/life coordination,
governed next action, and longitudinal write-back. A VHT component may
therefore be embedded as a high-value model within HFWM, while HFWM
supplies a common task-function-action interface across professional and
everyday contexts.

Neither WPP nor VHT is treated here as deficient. They address different
but overlapping integration problems. HFWM is proposed as a
complementary whole-person operational coordinate that can connect
multiscale biological models to what the same person must do, can do,
actually does, and should do next under evidence and authority
constraints.
