# 9. Bidirectional Reasoning Between Medicine and Human Function

## 9.1 Medicine to human function

Medical information enters HFWM as typed evidence and context.
Diagnosis, disease stage, injury, surgery, medication, imaging,
laboratory results, medical restrictions, red flags, and safety
clearance can constrain internal tasks, capacity, functional boundaries,
and admissible action. The model then asks what those medical facts mean
for the same person's current ability to sustain life, recover, move,
eat, communicate, work, exercise, and participate in real life.

## 9.2 Human function to medicine

The reverse direction is equally important. A new rapid decline in
previously stable function, progressive weakness, unexpected recovery
delay, an unexplained change in task tolerance, or a pattern that is no
longer explained by the current functional hypothesis can generate a
governed medical question. HFWM does not convert that signal into an
autonomous diagnosis. It escalates the need for medical reassessment,
preserves the evidence and uncertainty, and later incorporates the
resulting medical evidence back into the same person model.

## 9.3 The boundary

The relation is bidirectional but not symmetric equivalence. Medical
diagnosis is not human-function judgment, and human-function change is
not a disease diagnosis. Evidence, safety, professional authority, and
scope must mediate both directions.

```text
MEDICAL EVIDENCE
→ INTERNAL TASKS / SAFETY / CAPACITY
→ HUMAN-FUNCTION STATE
→ UNEXPECTED CHANGE / EVIDENCE GAP
→ MEDICAL QUESTION / REFERRAL
→ NEW MEDICAL EVIDENCE
```

# 10. Coordination Around the Whole Person

HFWM is not only a medical coordination model. The same person's state
may require coordination across professional care, caregiving,
self-management, exercise, nutrition, psychology, family, assistive
technology, and environment. The topology is dynamic: the current
leading role changes with the primary problem, task, evidence, risk,
professional scope, and stage of recovery or maintenance.

| Participant / system | Typical HFWM contribution |
|---|---|
| Physician | diagnosis, medical stability, disease/injury management, medical restrictions and escalation |
| Nurse | clinical monitoring, medication execution within nursing scope, wound/ongoing care, education, continuity |
| Physical therapist (physiotherapist in jurisdictions using that title) | mobility, movement-related capacities, load, task recovery, physical-function restoration within scope |
| Occupational therapist | self-care, domestic, work, role participation, environmental and assistive adaptation |
| Speech-language pathologist / speech and language therapist | communication, language, speech, swallowing and related functional tasks within qualification |
| Audiologist | hearing and auditory-function assessment and management within qualification |
| Dietitian | nutrition risk, intake, material and energy conditions, recovery/adaptation support within scope |
| Psychologist / psychotherapist | mental-health, cognitive-emotional-behavioral factors and therapy within qualification |
| Exercise professional | long-term capacity maintenance, development, load progression, performance and boundary expansion when safe |
| Massage/manual-practice professional | manual or massage-related services where legally authorized and evidence-appropriate |
| Person, family, caregiver | goals, preferences, self-management, daily observation, assistance, adherence, lived context |
| Environment and technology | home/work/school/community conditions, assistive devices, accessibility, task modification |

*Table 3. Illustrative coordination topology. Scope varies by jurisdiction, qualification, institution, and case; the table does not create professional authority.*

HFWM should therefore represent not a fixed linear pathway but a
governed routing problem: who should lead now, who should contribute
evidence, who may act, what question is being handed over, what
conditions require re-entry by another professional, and when the person
can return to self-management or lower-intensity maintenance.

# 11. Evidence, Uncertainty, and Judgment

A world model of a person cannot treat all information as equally
authoritative. HFWM preserves distinctions among underlying reality,
observed or reported evidence, measurement result, professional
interpretation, model hypothesis, predicted result, and confirmed
action. Missing evidence does not establish a negative fact. A
manifestation does not identify a unique cause. An abnormal test does
not automatically identify the primary limiting factor. An intervention
response updates support for a hypothesis but does not retrospectively
prove a unique cause [3].

The model therefore needs an evidence-conditioned belief state rather
than forced certainty. At the present research stage, qualitative
governed statuses such as supported, weakened, contradicted, not
assessed, insufficient, and undetermined are preferable to invented
numerical probabilities unless calibration data exist. Unknown is a
valid result.

Assessment is an information-acquisition action. Its value depends on
whether it can discriminate among currently plausible hypotheses and
change the next action. This principle supports minimal sufficient
assessment rather than indiscriminate testing.

# 12. Governed Action Selection and Responsibility Routing

HFWM's action space is broader than treatment. Candidate actions include
direct explanation, observation, waiting, evidence collection,
assessment, reassessment, environmental modification, demand reduction,
assistance, self-management, recovery support, medical escalation,
professional referral, physical therapy, occupational therapy,
speech-language or audiology services, psychological care, nutrition
support, exercise progression, maintenance, pause, stop, and monitoring.

An action becomes admissible only when its action-specific evidence
threshold, safety conditions, consent, professional authority, scope,
feasibility, stop conditions, and reassessment requirements are
satisfied. Lower-risk information-gathering actions may be admissible
precisely because evidence is insufficient for higher-consequence
action. This is a central safety property of the model.

For discharged patients, this produces an explicit continuity function.
Discharge ends one medical episode; it does not end the person model.
The model can distinguish states in which medical reassessment remains
primary, states appropriate for physical therapy or occupational
therapy, states requiring communication/swallowing or hearing services,
states in which nutrition or psychological support is materially
limiting, and states in which long-term exercise or self-management
becomes appropriate.

# 13. Longitudinal State Transition and Model Update

HFWM becomes a world model rather than a static ontology only when it
represents change. Let X_t denote the person's underlying whole-person
state, T_t the current internal/external task set, D_t the resulting
demand specification, e_t the Actual Engagement Process, u_t the
governed action or intervention, K_t the context and environment, H_t
the relevant history, and ω_t unobserved disturbances. Engagement and
action are kept as different typed variables. A general transition can
be written schematically as:

**X_{t+1} = F(X_t, T_t, D_t, e_t, u_t, K_t, H_t, ω_t)**

This equation is an architectural statement, not a universal biological
law. The actual person can change without being measured. Conversely,
the model representation can change because new evidence corrects a
previous belief even when the person's underlying state has not changed.
HFWM therefore separates actual transition from belief/model update.

**M_{t+1} = B(M_t, Y_{t+1}, E_{t+1}, provenance, ruleVersion)**

Historical states remain replayable. New evidence may support, weaken,
contradict, or leave unresolved previous judgments. A legitimate
longitudinal model never silently rewrites the past with the latest
interpretation.

The current framework supports governed state representation,
evidence-constrained reasoning, action routing, feedback, reassessment,
and longitudinal update. Quantitatively calibrated future-state
prediction, multi-step rollout, causal counterfactual simulation, and
autonomous optimization remain research targets that require additional
empirical validation.
