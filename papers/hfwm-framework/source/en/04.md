# 14. Body Function Report as a Human-Readable World-State Projection

A Body Function Report is not the world-model state itself. It is a
governed human-readable projection of the evidence-supported modeled
state for a defined audience and time. Formally:

**Report_t = Projection(ModelState_t, Evidence_t, Audience, Authority, Version)**

This distinction prevents a report from becoming a second source of
truth. The model may contain latent hypotheses, unresolved evidence,
historical versions, machine-only provenance, or information not
appropriate for a particular audience. A member-facing report should
communicate current functional state, principal manifestations,
meaningful changes, current boundaries, next actions, and follow-up
conditions in understandable language. A professional-facing projection
may additionally expose reasoning basis, evidence chains, uncertainty,
risk boundaries, unresolved questions, and reassessment criteria.

Across time, immutable report snapshots create an interpretable
longitudinal surface over the deeper model. The value is not merely
documentation. Reports allow the person, family, and multiple
professionals to orient to the same current state and understand why
responsibility or action changes. They also preserve a shared state
narrative across illness, treatment, discharge, restoration, long-term
maintenance, and future events.

Because the report is audience-specific, a physician, physical
therapist, occupational therapist, speech-language pathologist / speech
and language therapist, audiologist, dietitian, psychologist or
psychotherapist, exercise professional, and the person need not see
identical detail. They should, however, remain anchored to the same
underlying person identity, evidence snapshot, and versioned world
state.

# 15. Worked Cases

## 15.1 Internal-task case: infection and recovery

A person develops an acute infection. The infection is a medical event
and pathological context; it is not itself the internal task. Where the
corresponding governed task identities and trigger rules are
established, it may activate internal tasks involving defense, immune
regulation, thermal regulation, circulation and energy allocation,
tissue protection, recovery, and restoration of homeostasis. Evidence
may include symptoms, temperature, laboratory or clinical findings,
sleep, appetite, fatigue, cardiorespiratory response, and recovery
trajectory. The person's external work and mobility tasks may
simultaneously be reduced. HFWM represents both sets of demands,
preserves medical authority, and can route actions from monitoring and
rest to medical escalation depending on safety and evidence.

## 15.2 Discharge case: from medical stability to life restoration

Consider a person discharged after surgery. Medical discharge
establishes that a hospital episode has reached a defined transition
point; it does not establish complete human-function restoration. The
person may still have internal tasks of repair and recovery and external
tasks involving transfers, dressing, bathing, eating, mobility, stairs,
communication, work, caregiving, or return to exercise. A physical
therapist may lead mobility and load-related restoration; an
occupational therapist may lead self-care, domestic, work-role, or
environmental adaptation; a nurse may continue wound and care
continuity; a dietitian or other qualified nutrition professional may
become relevant to intake and recovery; a psychologist or
psychotherapist may enter when mental-health or behavioral factors
materially limit recovery; a speech-language pathologist/speech and
language therapist or audiologist may enter when indicated. The leading
role can change while the person model remains continuous.

## 15.3 Re-routing case: new risk during maintenance

A previously stable person in long-term exercise maintenance develops
new progressive weakness and a rapid decline in a previously reliable
task. HFWM should not reinterpret this automatically as poor exercise
adherence or a simple capacity fluctuation. The change weakens the
previous model, generates a safety-relevant evidence gap, and routes the
person back to medical assessment. New medical evidence is then
integrated into the same model before deciding whether the next stage is
medical treatment, physical therapy, occupational therapy, other
professional care, modified exercise, or observation.

# 16. Why HFWM Is a World Model

| World-model requirement | HFWM realization | Current evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| State | versioned whole-person state and evidence-supported representation | Formally specified |
| Observation | reported, observed, measured, device, and professional evidence | Formally specified |
| Memory | longitudinal history, prior states, actions, responses, unresolved uncertainty | Formally specified |
| Dynamics | actual change under internal/external tasks, endogenous regulation, environment, and action | Formal transition structure specified; not empirically calibrated |
| Action conditioning | governed actions influence future state and/or information | Formally specified with governance constraints |
| Uncertainty | explicit unknowns, competing hypotheses, evidence status | Formally specified |
| Planning | selection and routing of the next admissible action | Partial: governed next-action selection/routing, not optimal planning |
| Update | feedback and reassessment revise the model while preserving history | Formally specified with history preservation |
| Prediction | potential/predicted results represented separately; calibrated prediction is a staged research target | Represented as a distinct object; not empirically validated in this paper |

*Table 4. Why HFWM exceeds a static ontology, record system, report generator, or ordinary question-answering model.*

UOHF supplies the semantic coordinate system; HFWM uses that coordinate
system to maintain and update a modeled world around a specific person.
The distinction is analogous to the difference between defining the
entities and relations of a domain and maintaining a changing,
action-conditioned state of one concrete world instance.

# 17. Implementation-Oriented Architecture Without Commercial Disclosure

A public HFWM architecture can be described in functional layers without
publishing proprietary production logic. The minimum layers are: (1) a
governed semantic layer for person, task, demand, human-function
capacity, engagement, evidence, state, action, and provenance; (2) a
versioned human-function capacity coordinate system, currently
represented publicly by 18 core and 104 specific capacities [12]; (3)
a governed knowledge-relation layer, Functional Bridge, that can connect
those capacities to anatomical and spatial reference knowledge such as
HRA [13], multiscale physiological reference knowledge such as WPP
[5,6], and other controlled scientific sources while preserving
provenance and inference boundaries; (4) a person-state layer with
versioned longitudinal memory; (5) an evidence layer; (6) a reasoning
layer; (7) an action-governance and responsibility-routing layer; and
(8) a longitudinal update and report-projection layer.

The paper intentionally does not publish production prompts, provider
selection rules, context compression, private retrieval/reranking logic,
proprietary relation inventories, database schemas, billing rules, or
other implementation details that are not necessary to evaluate the
scientific framework. Scientific reproducibility should focus on public
definitions, formal objects, example cases, benchmark tasks, and
versioned public UOHF resources.

# 18. Formal Requirements Traceability, Competency Cases, and Empirical Research Program

## 18.1 Requirements-traceability design

A formal requirements-traceability analysis was performed against the
explicit world-model requirements defined in Section 16 and the worked
competency cases in Section 15. The analysis asks whether the framework
contains explicit typed carriers and governance rules for state,
observation, memory, dynamics, action conditioning, uncertainty,
planning/routing, update, and prediction, and whether representative
cases can be traversed without collapsing disease into task, process
into inference, functional evidence into diagnosis, or professional
routing into unauthorized action. This is an architectural and semantic
analysis, not a clinical outcome trial or a calibrated runtime
prediction benchmark.

| Criterion | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| State, observation, memory, uncertainty, update | SATISFIED at formal specification level | Explicit typed objects, provenance, versioning, and history-preserving update rules are defined. |
| Action conditioning | SATISFIED at formal specification level | Governed action is separated from bodily engagement and constrained by evidence, safety, authority, and reassessment. |
| Dynamics | SATISFIED at formal architecture level | A real-state transition relation is specified, but the transition function is not claimed to be a calibrated universal human equation. |
| Planning | PARTIAL | HFWM supports governed next-action selection and responsibility routing; optimal multi-step planning is not established. |
| Prediction | NOT EMPIRICALLY VALIDATED | Potential/predicted results are typed separately, but calibrated future-state prediction is outside the empirical claims of this paper. |

## 18.2 Worked competency-case analysis

The three worked cases were evaluated as competency tests of semantic
and authority consistency. They are not epidemiological samples; their
purpose is to test whether the framework preserves its own type and
governance commitments across materially different situations.

| Worked case | Competency tested | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Infection and recovery | Medical event remains distinct from governed internal task; internal task generates/grounds Demand; medical authority is preserved. | CONFORMS |
| Post-discharge restoration | Persistent person identity is preserved while internal recovery tasks, external life tasks, and multiple professional roles change over time. | CONFORMS |
| New risk during maintenance | New evidence can weaken a prior model and trigger medical reassessment without autonomously diagnosing disease. | CONFORMS |

The traceability analysis shows that the proposed framework explicitly
represents most stated world-model requirements at the formal
architecture level, while planning remains partial and prediction
remains unvalidated. These findings do not establish clinical
effectiveness, predictive superiority, or safety in unsupervised
deployment.

## 18.3 Empirical research program

- Semantic validity: experts can distinguish task, demand, capacity,
  actual engagement, manifestation, evidence, hypothesis, action, and
  state without category collapse.
- Task-demand validity: internal and external tasks can be mapped to
  defensible demand specifications and required functions.
- Evidence discipline: the system preserves insufficient evidence,
  avoids symptom-to-cause shortcuts, and can state what evidence is
  still needed.
- Professional/life routing validity: the next professional,
  self-management, caregiving, or environmental action is appropriate to
  evidence, safety, authority, and scope.
- Longitudinal consistency: new evidence updates the model without
  overwriting historical states or inventing continuity that was not
  observed.
- Decision utility: model-guided next actions improve safety,
  efficiency, information gain, continuity, or functional outcomes
  compared with simpler baselines.
- Report utility: person-facing and professional-facing projections
  improve understanding, handover quality, and continuity while
  preserving evidence boundaries.
- Predictive validity: when calibrated models become available,
  structured HFWM states outperform simpler variables for predicting
  deterioration, recovery, escalation, task persistence, or response.
- Generalization: the same core architecture remains stable across
  internal demands, daily living, communication, mobility, work,
  exercise, aging, disease events, and different professional settings.

A staged capability ladder is recommended. HFWM-L1: whole-person state
representation. HFWM-L2: evidence-constrained reasoning and routing.
HFWM-L3: validated state-transition prediction. HFWM-L4: causally
defensible counterfactual simulation and planning. The label HFWM should
not be used to imply that every implementation has reached L4.
