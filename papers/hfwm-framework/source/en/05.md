# 19. Falsifiability and Failure Conditions

- If capacity and actual engagement cannot be operationally distinguished across well-designed studies, the central two-axis claim has limited empirical value.
- If internal-task modeling adds no explanatory, predictive, or decision value beyond ordinary physiological or medical state descriptions, that extension should be narrowed.
- If professional routing cannot be made more reliable than simple referral rules while preserving safety and scope, its added complexity is unjustified.
- If longitudinal updates overwrite history or cannot explain why a state changed, the world-model claim is weakened.
- If person-facing reports routinely diverge from the evidence-supported modeled state, the projection layer has failed.
- If the model systematically turns manifestations, structures, or test results into unsupported causes or diagnoses, governance has failed.
- If the model cannot preserve explicit unknowns, it is not an evidence-constrained whole-person model.
- If future predictive claims do not outperform simpler baselines, predictive extensions should be rejected or revised.

# 20. Discussion

## 20.1 Human function as a whole-person organizing coordinate

HFWM proposes that the whole person can be modeled coherently without forcing every relevant fact into one discipline. Human function provides a common operational coordinate because every living person must continuously meet internal demands of life and external demands of living. Structure, physiology, disease, psychology, nutrition, environment, and professional action acquire a shared position when they are related to what the same person must do, can do, actually does, and becomes able or unable to do over time.

## 20.2 The model is broader than medicine

Although medicine is essential, HFWM is not a medical world model alone. It follows the person before disease, during disease and treatment, after discharge, through physical therapy, occupational therapy, communication or swallowing care, hearing care, nutrition and psychological support, exercise, self-management, family life, work, aging, and new events. The continuity of the person—not the continuity of a single professional service—is the organizing principle.

## 20.3 The model is not reductionist

Saying that the whole person is modeled through human function does not mean that every property of personhood is a human function. HFWM is a model of the person's bodily capacity to sustain life and act in the world, conditioned by values, preferences, relationships, environment, and professional context where relevant. The distinction protects both scientific clarity and the irreducibility of the person.

## 20.4 Relationship to WPP and VHT

WPP and European VHT efforts demonstrate the international movement toward integrated, multiscale and personalized models of the human body and health [5-10]. HFWM offers an additional organizing layer: internal/external tasks, demand-conditioned human-function capacities, actual engagement, professional/life routing, and longitudinal action-feedback. The long-term opportunity is interoperability rather than territorial competition.

# 21. Limitations

- The present paper reports a formal conformance evaluation and worked competency cases, but it is not a clinical validation study, clinical outcome benchmark, or calibrated predictive-model evaluation.
- Whole-person modeling does not mean exhaustive simulation of all biological variables; many relevant states remain latent or unmodeled.
- The current UOHF capacity catalogue and relation topology remain versioned scientific artifacts and may evolve.
- Professional scopes vary by jurisdiction and institution; HFWM routing cannot create legal authority.
- Causal claims require appropriate evidence and study design; repeated improvement after action does not prove a unique cause.
- Predictive and counterfactual capabilities require calibrated longitudinal and intervention data and are not implied by the present architecture.
- The global-priority claim is necessarily provisional and should be updated if earlier equivalent work is identified.
- This work originates within an organization developing related software, ontology assets, training, and services; independent evaluation is necessary.

# 22. Conclusion

The Human Function World Model is proposed as a whole-person world model whose enduring subject is the same person and whose organizing coordinate is human function. In its current operational profile, governed internal-task representations connect internal goals such as maintenance, defense, repair, recovery, and adaptation to Demand, while external tasks connect self-care, communication, mobility, work, family life, social participation, and exercise to their demands. HFWM connects structure and physiology with disease and injury, functional capacity with the Actual Engagement Process and evidence-bearing engagement assertions, evidence with uncertainty, medicine with human-function reassessment, professional care with self-management and environment, action with feedback, and current state with longitudinal change.

The central claim is not that one equation already simulates the complete human body. It is that one coherent, evidence-constrained and versioned world-model architecture can keep the same whole person intact while different biological scales, professions, life contexts, actions, and time points enter the model. Human function remains the body's capacity to be appropriately engaged to meet internal and external demands; HFWM uses that capacity-centered concept as the unified operational coordinate for modeling how the same whole person sustains life and lives in the world.

If validated, HFWM could provide a shared computational foundation for continuity from disease and medical treatment through discharge, restoration of daily life, physical and occupational therapy, communication and hearing care, nutrition and psychological support, exercise, maintenance, and future health events—without turning those domains into one profession or one undifferentiated model.
